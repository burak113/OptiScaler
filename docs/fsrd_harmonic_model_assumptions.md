The separate uncertainty experiment assumes known IID RGB noise, eight independent past frames, per-frame physical coefficients/intercepts, and shared continuous frequencies. It evaluates the local Gaussian Jacobian at known oracle frequencies and amplitudes; it does not estimate frequencies from observations. Centered cos/sin derivatives agree with finite differences to 3.4041e-11. The Schur frequency covariance agrees with the corresponding block of the full joint inverse Fisher matrix to relative error 4.4856e-16. The conditional circular variance envelope dominates the fixed-frequency real coefficient covariance to numerical precision.

After a fresh current-frame coefficient refit, omitted frequency uncertainty adds about 3.78% to 3.94% of conditional atom variance in the nondegenerate cases. Close frequencies give oracle frequency standard errors of approximately 0.019 to 0.038 cycles across the patch, compared with approximately 0.0013 for a separated ordinary wave. At exact Nyquist the retained physical basis is intercept plus sine; first-order frequency information is singular. This is not zero frequency uncertainty and must not be inverted as a regular model.

The companion static-history calculation holds the learned frequency error common while averaging independent current coefficients. Conditional coefficient variance falls with observation count, while the common frequency contribution does not. For the one-wave example at 56 current coefficient observations, its common-frequency atom variance is 2.2068 times the conditional mean variance. This is a component-level local oracle calculation, not final-pilot noise: the residual subtracts the current fitted atom, so its common error is anticorrelated and can cancel in a consistent reconstruction. Selected-frequency error, correlations, residual filtering, phase/innovation decisions, and model activation require actual candidate measurements.

The experiments support testing the residual and learned-frequency uncertainty explicitly.
